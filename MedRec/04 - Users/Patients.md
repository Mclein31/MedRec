# Patients

Patients represent the primary user group and core focus of MedRec. In the existing healthcare ecosystem, patients are typically treated as passive subjects whose records are distributed across fragmented institutional databases rather than active owners of their health data.

The premise of [[MedRec Vision]] is that individuals must have sovereign ownership, seamless portability, and clear comprehension of their own [[Medical Records]].

## Core Characteristics

- **Primary Stakeholder**: Patients are the primary actors initiating record uploads, organization, and sharing permissions.
- **Heterogeneous Technical Literacy**: Users range from digital natives managing chronic conditions via smartphones to elderly individuals or non-technical users needing straightforward interfaces.
- **High-Stakes Context**: Interacting with medical records frequently happens during stressful life events (new diagnoses, specialist visits, hospital transitions, emergencies).

## Key Relationships & Dynamics

- **To Needs**: Grounded in [[User Needs]]—specifically immediate access, portability across providers, clarity of content, and strict privacy.
- **To Frustrations**: Experiencing direct friction documented in [[User Pain Points]] and [[Patient Frustrations]], such as repetitive intake forms and inaccessible legacy portals.
- **To Personas**: Archetypes and specific behavioral profiles are being defined in [[User Personas]].

## Current Working Assumptions

> [!NOTE]
> **Assumption**: Patients want consolidated ownership of their health history on their personal devices rather than relying on fragmented, provider-specific web portals.
> 
> **Assumption**: Patients are willing to spend a minimal amount of time organizing or capturing records if it saves them significant friction during doctor visits.
> 
> *These assumptions require validation through targeted user research interviews.*

## Open Questions

- What is the primary trigger that drives a patient to seek a personal medical record management app (e.g., chronic illness diagnosis, moving cities, pregnancy, elder care)?
- How comfortable are patients with handling raw diagnostic files (e.g., lab PDFs, DICOM scans) versus simplified summaries?
- What are the boundary lines where patients feel overwhelmed by medical terminology?
