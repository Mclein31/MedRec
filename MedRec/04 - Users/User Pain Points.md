# User Pain Points

This note details the core pain points and friction areas identified during initial problem exploration. These represent the primary frustrations experienced by patients navigating modern healthcare systems.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Status**: All pain points listed below are **assumptions** formulated from initial observations. They serve as hypotheses to be tested during user research via [[Research Questions]].

## Assumed Pain Points

### 1. Inability to Retrieve Records from Prior Providers (Assumed)
- **Assumption**: When patients switch clinics, move to a new city, or change insurance networks, retrieving past medical records from former providers is extremely tedious, slow, or sometimes impossible.
- **Root Cause**: Fragmented health information systems, lack of interoperability, and cumbersome institutional release-of-information protocols.
- **Impact**: Patients start from scratch with new doctors, leading to redundant diagnostic procedures and delayed care.

### 2. Disparate and Incompatible Record Formats (Assumed)
- **Assumption**: Patients receive medical data scattered across paper printouts, low-resolution scanned images, password-protected PDFs, proprietary portal messages, and physical CD-ROMs (radiology).
- **Root Cause**: Absence of a unified consumer export standard across legacy medical systems.
- **Impact**: Patients cannot easily aggregate or view their complete health timeline in a single format.

### 3. Repetitive Intake Information Entry (Assumed)
- **Assumption**: Patients are forced to repeatedly fill out identical paper intake forms detailing their medical history, surgeries, allergies, and current medications at every new clinic or doctor visit.
- **Root Cause**: Clinic intake workflows do not integrate with external health records.
- **Impact**: High cognitive fatigue, user frustration, and risk of omission of critical allergies or medication details.

### 4. Lack of Visibility / Ignorance of Own Record Holdings (Assumed)
- **Assumption**: Most individuals do not know exactly what medical records exist about them, where they are stored, or whether their health history is complete and up to date.
- **Root Cause**: Records are sequestered across separate hospital databases without a centralized personal dashboard.
- **Impact**: Patients feel powerless and unable to advocate for themselves during critical medical consultations.

### 5. High Anxiety Regarding Health Privacy & Security (Assumed)
- **Assumption**: Patients fear that storing their sensitive health data digitally makes them vulnerable to data leaks, surveillance, or predatory third-party commercialization (e.g., insurers adjusting premiums).
- **Root Cause**: High-profile healthcare data breaches and dark patterns in digital health services.
- **Impact**: Skepticism toward adoption of cloud-based health tools without strong privacy assurances and local-first or zero-knowledge protections.
